# Pre-registration — OpenHear environmental awareness pilot

**Status:** Template. Not yet submitted to a registry (for example OSF or ClinicalTrials.gov). No participants have been enrolled under this protocol. Ethics approval, a university or NHS sponsor, and a named clinical lead are still required before any data collection.

**Canonical claim:** The only intended-purpose statement is in [docs/INTENDED_PURPOSE.md](docs/INTENDED_PURPOSE.md).

> OpenHear is a research platform that turns the wearer's own audiogram into haptic patterns for environmental sound awareness. It is not a certified medical device, not a hearing aid, and not a replacement for clinical care.

---

## 1. Background and rationale

People with severe or profound hearing loss, and people who remove their hearing aids, often miss everyday environmental sounds (a knock, an alarm, a child calling from another room, traffic) when aids are out or the phone is out of reach. Phone alerts require the phone to be in range. Fixed transmitters require one device per source.

OpenHear maps a small set of recognised sound classes to short, distinct vibration patterns on the wrist. The patterns are weighted by the person's own audiogram. Recognition and the audiogram stay on the device. Nothing is sent to a cloud. The claim is awareness only.

## 2. Objectives

**Primary objective**

To measure whether participants can detect and correctly identify a small set of everyday environmental sound classes from short, distinct wrist vibration patterns, when the patterns have been weighted by their own audiogram.

**Secondary objectives**

- Safety and comfort of continuous-wear vibration patterns during ordinary use.
- Independence: whether participants can use the system without continuous support from another person.
- Preference for on-device storage of the audiogram versus any alternative.

## 3. Design

Within-subject. Each participant is their own control. No comparison against amplification or against a hearing aid on speech-in-noise tests. Those tests measure a different claim.

## 4. Participants

**Inclusion**

- Severe-to-profound hearing loss (pure-tone average ≥ 71 dB HL in the better ear), or
- People who regularly remove their hearing aids for part of the day, regardless of pure-tone average.

**Exclusion**

- Mild or moderate loss on its own (pure-tone average < 71 dB HL) where the person does not remove their aids.
- Inability to give informed consent.
- Skin condition on the wrist that would make continuous vibration unsafe.

Recruitment via RNID Research Panel volunteers who have already consented in writing to testing, and via any university or NHS partner that becomes the sponsor. Exact numbers to be set with the sponsor and ethics committee.

## 5. Intervention

OpenHear software running on a personal phone or on the wristband (when available). The classes the code supports are: voice, doorbell, alarm, dog, traffic, media, knock, baby_cry, phone. These are recognised on-device and mapped to short vibration patterns. Patterns are weighted by the participant's own audiogram, which remains on the device. No cloud upload of audio or audiogram unless the participant explicitly chooses to paste it into a tool they already use.

## 6. Outcomes

**Primary outcomes**

- Detection rate: proportion of target sound-class events that produce a correct haptic response within a pre-specified window.
- Identification rate: proportion of detected events that are correctly named by the participant from a closed set of classes.

**Secondary outcomes**

- Adverse events related to vibration (skin irritation, discomfort, pain).
- Participant-reported independence in ordinary environments.
- Time to learn the pattern set to a pre-specified criterion.

## 7. Analysis plan

Descriptive statistics for detection and identification rates, with confidence intervals. No superiority claim against a hearing aid. Safety events listed in full. Analysis plan to be finalised with the sponsor and statistician before data collection begins. Any deviation from this plan will be recorded.

## 8. Data and privacy

Audiogram and recognition stay on the device by default. Only the minimum data required for the study (for example anonymised detection scores and adverse-event reports) will leave the device, under the sponsor's data-management plan and with explicit consent. Raw audio is not stored as a clinical fact.

## 9. Ethics and governance

- Ethics approval from a recognised research ethics committee is required before enrolment.
- A university or NHS organisation must act as sponsor.
- A named clinical lead is required.
- Consent form currently contains the placeholder "[REC name TBC]". This will be replaced before any use.
- Product-liability cover and any university or contract-facility requirements will be addressed with the sponsor.
